# Prepare a public talk from a research paper

## Goal

Give a 30-minute talk at a cultural centre ("Pint of Science" style) about our recent paper on gravitational waves, for an adult audience with no science background.

## What I did

1. **Uploaded the paper** and described the audience:

    > *I'll give a 30-minute talk about this paper to adults with no science background, in a bar, in Catalan. Propose a storyline: an opening hook, three key ideas maximum, and an ending that people remember. No equations.*

2. **Asked for analogies and checked them:**

    > *Give me five everyday analogies for how a gravitational-wave detector works. For each, say where the analogy breaks down.*

    Kept two; discarded the ones that were misleading.

3. **Built the slide plan:** *"Turn the storyline into 15 slides: title, one sentence of text maximum, and what image to show."* Made the slides myself in our usual software with real images (ESA/NASA/LIGO public images, credited).

4. **Prepared for questions:** *"What are the ten questions a curious non-specialist is most likely to ask after this talk? Draft a short, honest answer to each."*

5. **Rehearsed** by pasting my spoken script and asking: *"Where would a non-specialist get lost? Which sentences are too long to say out loud?"*

## Result

A clear storyline, a slide plan, two good analogies, and a list of likely questions. The talk itself, the images and the delivery remained mine.

## What to watch for

- **Analogies can mislead.** Always ask where they break down.
- **Simplification vs. error**: check that simplified statements are still true.
- **Images**: Claude can't provide usable images; use public agency images with credits, or your own.
- **Don't let it sound generic.** Add your own stories: why you work on this, what surprised you.
