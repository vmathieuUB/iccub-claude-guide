# Organise a seminar series

!!! info "Illustrative example"
    This example was written by the site editors to show the workflow. It is not yet a real ICCUB experience. If you try it, [send us your version](../../contribute/index.md) and we will replace it.

| **Tools used** | A Project, Gmail and Google Calendar connectors |
| **Time saved** | A few hours per term (estimate) |

## Goal

Run the term's group seminar series: invite speakers, find dates, send announcements, and keep a schedule, with less back-and-forth email.

## What I did

1. **Created a Project** "Seminars 2026–27" with the list of possible speakers, the room booking rules, and last year's announcement emails as style examples. Instructions: *"Announcements in English, short, with title, abstract, speaker, date, room 503, and Zoom link placeholder."*

2. **Found dates**, with the calendar connector:

    > *Find all Wednesdays at 12:00 between 14 October and 16 December when I have nothing in my calendar. Exclude the week of 2 November.*

3. **Drafted invitations**, one per speaker, adapted to each: *"Draft an invitation to each speaker on the list, proposing two of the free dates, mentioning their recent paper on arXiv if you can find it."* Sent them myself after reading.

4. **Tracked replies:** later, *"Check my email for replies to the seminar invitations and update the schedule table. Who hasn't answered?"*

5. **Created calendar events and announcements** once a speaker confirmed: *"Create the calendar event and draft the announcement for the ICCUB mailing list from their title and abstract in their email."*

## Result

A full term's schedule set up in two short sessions, consistent announcements, and a table showing who still needed a reminder.

## What to watch for

- **Check the speaker information** (affiliation, title, paper) before it goes into an announcement.
- **Calendar actions**: Claude asks before creating events. Read the date, time and time zone carefully, especially for remote speakers.
- **Send invitations yourself.** A personal note from you works better than a perfect template.
